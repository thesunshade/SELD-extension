3. THE ‘MUST’ OR ‘HAVE TO CONSTRUCTION: direct case… onnə ඔන්න

3.1 In the previous conversation, onnə ඔන්න appeared with the equivalent of the English subject in the dative case and the meaning ‘want In one of the sentences in the present conversation, onnə ඔන්න appears in a construction in which it means ‘must, have to’:

Tæksiyəkiŋ yannə oonə
ටැක්සියකින් යන්න ඕන
You must go by a taxi.

This construction differs from the ‘want’ construction in two principal ways:

1. It occurs with the infinitive of a verb only, never with a dependent noun. Here a comparison with English may help. Tə … onnə ට … ඔන්න ‘want’, like English ‘want (to)’ may take either a noun or a verb. oonə ඕන ‘must’ may take only a verb, like English ‘must’ or ‘have to’.

2. When the equivalent of the English subject is expressed, it is in the direct case, not in the dative. Compare the following pairs:

maTə yannə oonǝ.
මට යන්න ඕන .
I want to go.

mamə yannə oonə
මම යන්න ඕන
I must go!

mahatteaTə paləturu gannə oonǝ.
මහත්තයට පලතුරු ගන්න ඕන .
The gentleman wants to buy fruit.

mahattea paləturu gannə oonǝ.
මහත්තයා පලතුරු ගන්න ඕන .
The gentleman must buy fruit.

3.2 In either of the two constructions with onnə ඔන්න, the equivalent of the English subject may be omitted when the situation makes the meaning clear. Thus

Tæksiyəkin yannə oonə
ටැක්සියකින් යන්න ඕන

could mean either ‘(I, you, he, she, they) must go by taxi.’ or ‘(I, you, he, she, they) want to go by taxi’, depending on the context or situation.

4. THE CAN CONSTRUCTION - puluwaŋ පුලුවන්

4.1 puluwaŋ පුලුවන් ‘can’ takes the form puluwəni පුලුවනි when the assertion marker is added. Either form may occur, optionally, at the end of statement sentences. Thus either

[page 42]

maTə puluwaŋ or
මට පුලුවන්
maTə puluwəni
මට පුලුවනි
I can

As with other forms that take the assertion marker, -y -යි is never present when də ද is added to form a question. Thus only

maTə puluwaŋ də?
මට පුලුවන් ද ?
Can I?

4.2 The puluwaŋ පුලුවන් ‘can’ construction, except for the possible occurrence of the assertion marker, is parallel in form to the onnə ඔන්න ‘want’ construction:

A. The equivalent of the English subject, when expressed, is in the dative case:

maTə Tæksiyak nawattannə puluwəni (or puluwaŋ).
මට ටැක්සියක් නවත්තන්න පුලුවනි (or පුලුවන් ).
I can stop a taxi.

mahatteaTə yannə puluwani (or puluwaŋ).
මහත්තයට යන්න පුලුවනි (or පුලුවන් ).
The gentleman can go!

B. It may occur with a verb in the infinitive, which may have its own object and modifiers:

mahatteaTə sigəræT bonnə puluwəni.
මහත්තයට සිගරැට් බොන්න පුලුවනි .
The gentleman can smoke cigarettes.

maTə kalutərəTə yannə puluwəni.
මට කලුතරට යන්න පුලුවනි .
I can go to Kalutara.

C. It may occur with a dependent noun in the direct case, although the nouns that may occur in this fashion are relatively few in number, such as siŋhələ සිංහල ‘Sinhalese’:

maTə siŋhələ puluwəni.
මට සිංහල පුලුවනි .
I know (literally ‘can’) Sinhalese.

5. THE QUESTION MARKER də ද WITH DIFFERENT SENTENCE TYPES

5.1 As pointed out before, də ද may be added to a simple statement converting it to a question. The simple statement always has the simple form of the verb, commonly occurring at the end, and the də ද is added immediately after the verb form.

Emphatic sentences use the emphatic form of a verb. In this case, də ද occurs immediately after the emphasized item in the sentence.

[page 43]

Most interrogatives will always be the emphasized word in any question in which they appear, and will therefore occur with the emphatic form of the verb and with də ද immediately following the interrogative.

SIMPLE SENTENCES

Statement:

mahattea kalutərəTə yanawa.
මහත්තයා කලුතරට යනවා .
The gentleman is going to Kalutara.

mahattea kalutərəTə yanawa də?

Is the gentleman going to Kalutara?

Statement:

mahattea yanne kalutərəTə. or
මහත්තයා යන්නෙ කලුතරට .
mahattea kalutərəTə yanne.

6.2 In quantity phrases, there is a general agreement between the use of the Sinhalese definite form and the English definite article, and the use of the Sinhalese indefinite form and the English form without any article:

hætæpmə tunak
හැතැප්ම තුනක්
three miles

hætæpmə tunə
හැතැප්ම තුන
the three miles

1. When a quantity expression is modified by a form like ‘this, that’, then the definite form is used:

mee sigəræT tunə
මේ සිගරැට් තුන
these three cigarettes

mahattea arə sigəræT pækæT tunə gannawa.
මහත්තයා අර සිගරැට් පැකැට් තුන ගන්නවා .
The gentleman is buying those three packs of cigarettes.

[page 46]

2. Quantity phrases as subjects or objects of simple sentences use the definite form where English uses the definite ‘the’ and the indefinite form where English uses no article:

bas hatərə yanəwa.
බස් හතර යනව .
The four buses are going.

bas hatǝrak yanəwa.
බස් හතරක් යනවා .
Four buses are going.

mahattea pækæT tunə gannawa.
මහත්තයා පැකැට් තුන ගන්නවා .
The gentleman is buying the three packs.

mahattea pækæT tunak gannəwa.
මහත්තයා පැකැට් තුනක් ගන්නවා .
The gentleman is buying three packs.

3. In emphatic sentences, the definite form of quantity phrases is used where the English would use the definite form:

bas hatərə yanne kalutərəTə.
බස් හතර යන්නෙ කලුතරට .
The four buses are going to Kalutara.

kalutərəTə yanne bas hatərə.
කලුතරට යන්නෙ බස් හතර .
It is the four buses that are going to Kalutara.

However, where English would use the form without an article, in Sinhala the indefinite is used if it is not the emphasized word, but the definite with -y -යි if it is the emphasized word in final position. In the latter case, since the form in final position is emphasized, it is likely to equate with English ‘only such and such’:

bas hatǝrak yanne kalutərəTə.
බස් හතරක් යන්නෙ කලුතරට .
Four buses are going to Kalutara.

kalutərəTə yanne bas hatǝray.
කලුතරට යන්නෙ බස් හතරයි .
(Only) four buses are going to Kalutara.

4. Each of the types of corresponding question, with rules: sentences above has its də ද following the regular

bas hatǝrak yanəwa.
බස් හතරක් යනවා .
Four buses are going.

bas hatǝrak yanəwa də?
බස් හතරක් යනවා ද ?
Are four buses going?

7. kiiyak කීයක්

The interrogative word kiiyak කීයක් ‘how many’ behaves like a quantity word. Note that the form kiiyak කීයක් is the indefinite form of kiiyə කීය ‘how much, how many’. It is also special in that it is one of the few interrogatives, characteristically expressing quantity, which will not necessarily occur as the emphasized word immediately followed by də ද:

[page 47]

hætəpmə kiiyak tiyenəwa də?
හැතප්ම කීයක් තියෙනවා ද ?
How many miles is it?

æpəl kiiyak oonə də?
ඇපල් කීයක් ඕන ද ?
How many apples do you want?

8. Tikak ටිකක් and huŋ̆gak හුඟක්

A. Tikak ටිකක් is actually an indefinite form. The definite is Tikə ටික.

When it is used as a quantity word, meaning ‘some, (a) little, (a) small amount’, it acts like a numeral, and takes the same endings:

paləturu Tikak gannə onnə.
පලතුරු ටිකක් ගන්න ඔන්න .
I want to buy a little (some) fruit.

mee paləturu Tikə dennə.
මේ පලතුරු ටික දෙන්න .
Give me this small amount of fruit.

mehee tiyenne paləturu Tikay.
මෙහේ තියෙන්නෙ පලතුරු ටිකයයි .
Here there is (only) a little fruit.

(When the assertion marker is used with Tika ටික, ‘only’ is usually implied.)

B. huŋ̆gak හුන්ගක් ‘a lot, much’ is also used as a quantity word:

mehee paləturu huŋ̆gak tiyenəwa.
මෙහේ පලතුරු හුඟක් තියෙනවා .
There is a lot of fruit here.

However, the definite huŋ̆gə හුඟ is not used in sentences parallel to those above where the definite form Tika ටික is used.

C. Both indefinite forms, Tikak ටිකක් and huŋ̆gak හුඟක්, are used to qualify following adjectives to mean ‘somewhat, a little, a bit’ and ‘very, quite’ respectively. Thus:

huŋ̆gak durə də?
හුඟක් දුර ද ?
(Is it) very far?

Tikak duray.
ටිකක් දුරය් .
Somewhat distant.

D. huŋ̆gak හුඟක් and bohomə බොහොම

Notice that bohomə බොහොම and huŋ̆gak හුඟක් are commonly used inter-changeably to qualify adjectives.

bohomə rasə keselgeDi or
බොහොම රස කෙසෙල්ගෙඩි or
huŋ̆gak rasə keselgeDi
හුඟක් රස කෙසෙල්ගෙඩි
very tasty bananas

mee suruTTu bohomə særay. or
මේ සුරුට්ටු බොහොම සැරයි .
mee suruTTu huŋ̆gak særay.
මේ සුරුට්ටු හුඟක් සැරයි .
These cigars are very strong.

[page 48]

9. INDIRECT QUESTIONS

An indirect question can be formed from a direct question by the addition of kiyannə කියන්න ‘tell me.’

Direct

Tæksiyak ganne kohomə də?
ටැක්සියක් ගන්නෙ කොහොම ද ?
How does one get a taxi?

Indirect

Tæksiyak ganne kohomə də kiyannə.
ටැක්සියක් ගන්නෙ කොහොම ද කියන්න .
Tell me how to get a taxi.

Direct

mahattea yanne kohaaTə də?
මහත්තයා යන්නෙ කොහාට ද ?
Where is the gentleman going?

Indirect

mahattea yanne kohaaTə də kiyannə.
මහත්තයා යන්නෙ කොහාට ද කියන්න .
Tell me where the gentleman is going.

10.3 Use of the sets

Both mehee මෙහේ and metənə මෙතන specify location. The difference between them is one of specificity of location, metənə මෙතන indicating a more specific or smaller area than mehee මෙහේ. The difference does not normally show up in translation, although metənə මෙතන may sometimes be translated as ‘this spot’:

mehee in̆dələ koləm̆bəTə bas tiyenəwa də?
මෙහේ ඉඳල කොලඹට බස් තියෙනව ද ?
Is there a bus from here to Colombo?

metənə in̆dələ hætæpmə tunak tiyenəwə.
මෙතන ඉඳල හැතැප්ම තුනක් තියෙනව .
It is three miles from here (this spot).
