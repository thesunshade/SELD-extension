3. ඇත්තෙ

ඇති 'might be’ has an emphatic form ඇත්තෙ, which must be used in emphatic sentences. Compare the following pairs:

Simple:

මහත්තයා යනවා ඇති .
The master might go.

Emphatic:

යනවා ඇත්තේ මහත්තයා .
It is the master who might be going.

Simple:

මම ලඟ ත්‍රීරෝස් සිගරැට් ඇති .
I might have Three Roses cigarettes (in my shop).

[page 51]

Emphatic

මම ලඟ ඇත්තෙ ත්‍රීරෝස් සිගරැට් .

5.1 Verbal Nouns in -iimə or -illə:

A verbal noun is formed from the past tense stem of Classes 1 and 3 by dropping the final -u- or -un- of the past tense stem and adding the suffix -iimə or -illə. From verbs of Class 2 a verbal noun is formed by adding the same suffix -iimə or -illə to the past tense stem, but without the doubling of the last consonant of the stem.

6. REPETITION OF ADJECTIVES

6.1 Adjectives preceding a noun may be repeated (’reduplicated’) to give a sense like that of English ‘various’ plus adjective.

[page 56]

The noun must be plural:

හොඳ හොඳ පොත්
various good books

රස රස කෑම
various tasty foods

පුරාණ පුරාන සිද්දස්තාන
various ancient holy places

පොත් ලියන කොට වෙනස් වෙනස් අදහස් වෙනස් වෙනස් වීදිවලට ප්‍රකාස කරන්න පුලුවන් , නේ ?
When you are writing books, various different ideas can be expressed in various different ways, can't they?

Predicate adjectives are never reduplicated.

6.2 Verbal adjectives, in any tense, may also be reduplicated. The usual English equivalent is 'every…'. The noun may be either singular definite or plural with no change in meaning:

මිනිහා ලියන ලියන පොත හොඳයි .
Every book he writes is good.

ඒ ලමයා කරන කරන වැඩ මටත් කරන්න පුලුවනි .
Every job that boy does I can do, too.

මම බැලුව බැලුව තැන ලස්සනයි .
Every place I saw was beautiful.

නෝනා හදපු හදපු කරිය කට දැවා .
Every curry she made was hot.

7. 'FROM DAY TO DAY'

'From day to day', 'house to house', etc., may be expressed in Sinhalese with the indefinite of the noun plus ගානෙ (25,10).

දවසක් ගානෙ
from day to day

ගෙයක් ගාන
from house to house

Sinhalese has another way of expressing this. A noun in the instrumental case is followed by the dative case form of the same noun:

ගෙයින් ගෙට
from house to house

හන්දියෙන් හන්දියට
from corner to corner

ගහින් ගහට
from tree to tree

පැයෙන් පැයට
from hour to hour

[page 57]

However, if the noun involved refers to a period of time of a day or longer, the instrumental form of the noun is followed by the direct form:

දවසින් දවස
from day to day

සුමානෙන් සුමානෙ
from week to week

අවුරුද්දෙන් අවුරුද්ද
from year to year

8. ‘TWO FIFTY’

The Sinhalese equivalent of ‘two fifty’, ‘three forty’ etc., in counting money, quoting prices and telling time is expressed by adding a to the first numeral:

දෙකයි පනහ
two fifty (two rupees and fifty cents or 2:50 o’clock)

තුනයි හතලිහ
three forty

These phrases take case and definiteness endings and the assertion marker in the same way as single numerals:

දාහතරයි දහයයි .
Its (price is) fourteen ten.

දැන් තුනයි හතලිහයි .
It’s now 3:40 o’clock.

දෙකයි පනහට එන්න .
Come at 2:50.

මහත්තයා හතරයි විසි පහක් දුන්නා .
The master gave me four (rupees and) twenty-five (cents).

When it is necessary to specify the units of money involved, quantity phrases with the appropriate noun may be joined in the same way as numerals alone:

රුපියල් දෙකයි සත පනහක් දෙන්න .
Give two rupees and fifty cents.

මේක රුපියල් හතරයි සත දහයයි .
This one is four rupees and ten cents.

ගුනසිංහ රුපියල් දාහතරයි සත පනහක බඩු ගත්තා .
Gunasinha bought fourteen rupees and fifteen cents worth of goods.

[page 58]

9. ‘BEFORE, AFTER’

The postpositions ඉස්සෙල්ලා: ‘before’ and ‘after’ are used with a dependent noun or pronoun in the dative case (14, 9; 16, 9; 20, 10; 23, 7):

වැස්සට ඉස්සර ගෙදර යන්න .
Go home before the rain.

ලිවීමට ??? ඉස්සෙල්ලා කල්පනා කරන්න .
Think (about it) before you write.

මම ගියේ මහත්තයාට පස්සේ .
I went after you.

වැට බැඳිල්ලට පස්සේ මම වැඩ කරන්න ගියා .
After building the fence, I went to work.

While පස්සෙ may be used with a verbal noun, as above, it is more customary to use it with the dative case of the simple past tense of a verb, with no difference in meaning:

වැට බැන්දාට පස්සෙ මම වැඩ කරන්න ගියා .
After building the fence, I went to work.

Similarly, ඉස්සෙල්ලා and ඉස්සර are more commonly used with a dependent infinitive than they are with a verbal noun:

ලියන්න ඉස්සෙල්ලා කල්පනා කරන්න .
Think (about it) before you write.

ලංකාවට යන්න ඉස්සර සිංහල ටිකක් ඉගන ගන්න ඕන .
You should learn some Sinhalese before going to Ceylon.

10. ‘WAY, MANNER’

The form විදිය ‘the way, manner’ is used in the dative case where English uses ‘in a way’:

මේ විදියට වැඩ කරන්න එපා .
Don't work in this way.

මම ලියන විදියට ලියන්න බැරි ද ?
Can't you write the way I write?

11. ‘AS’ හැටියට

හැටියට equates with English ‘as, in the capacity of’:

මිනි හා ගුරුවරයෙක් හැටියට වැඩියෙ ප්‍රසිද්දයි .
He is more famous as a teacher.

[page 59]

ඩ්‍රයිවර් කෙනෙක් හැටියට වැඩ කරන්න පුලුවන් ද ?
Can you work as a driver?

12. NEGATIVE

For some adjectives a negative form is made by prefixing අ .

There are sometimes changes in the stem when this prefix is added:

කැමති

like

අකමැති

don’t like, dislike

සන්තොස

pleased

අසන්තොස

not pleased

ප්‍රයෝජනවත්

useful

අප්‍රයෝජනවත්

not useful

13. මං

මං is an alternative form for මම ‘I’. It generally implies greater informality:

මම යනවා .
මං යනවා .
I am going.

14. ‘I THINK ...’

In Sinhalese, both මං හිතන්නෙ (or මම හිතන්නෙ) and මගේ කල්පනාව equate with English ‘I think (that); my idea is that…’ The subordinate clause may follow these expressions without any introductory conjunction:

මං හිතන්නෙ උන්නැහෙ ගුරුවරයෙක් .
I think he’s a teacher.

මිනිහාගෙ කල්පනාව වැඩ නොකර ගෙදර යන්න .
His idea is to go home without doing work.

15. RESPECTFUL FORMS

The form වරයා (plural වරු) may be added to many nouns to make a term of respect. When this is added to a noun, it is the වරයා that is in inflected, and not the form to which it is added. Some nouns never occur without this suffix; for example, ‘teacher’, since this is always used as a term of respect. In other cases, the plural වරු may be added to a noun to form an alternative respectful plural even though it is not customary to use වරයා in the singular; for example දොස්තර 'doctor'

[page 60]

දොස්තරලා or දොස්තරවරු. The noun හාමුදුරුවො monk' always has the plural form හාමුදුරුවරු
