    1. Grammar

1. NOUN AND PRONOUN FORMS

Sinhalese nouns and pronouns may appear in several forms. They may be DEFINITE or INDEFINITE, SINGULAR or PLURAL, and inflected for any one of four CASES.

2. THE SINGULAR THE DEFINITE AND INDEFINITE FORMS

2.1 The definite indefinite distinction occurs only in the singular. bas-ekə බස්-එක ‘the bus’ and TikaT-ekə ටිකට්-එක the ticket’ are singular definite forms. The singular Indefinite forms are bas-ekak බස්-එකක් and TikæT-ekak ටිකැට්-එකක්. This type of definite and indefinite, with -ekə -එක and ekak එකක්, is often found with nouns such as these, which have been borrowed from English. rupiyələ රුපියල ‘the rupee’ has the indefinite form rupiyəlak රුපියලක්. Notice that these forms are very similar to those above. The only difference is that there is no -ek- -එක්- before the a අ definite and ak අක් indefinite.

Place names like koləm̆bə කොලඹ ‘Colombo’ and kalutərə කලුතර ‘Kalutara’ and pronouns like meeke මේකෙ ‘this one’ do not have indefinite forms.

2.2 In general, the definite form corresponds to English “the” with a noun, and the indefinite to English “a” with a noun. We say “in general” because this provides only a rough guide for the beginner: the forms do not always appear where the corresponding English translations would lead us to expect them. For example, the definite form appears after mee මේ ‘this’: mee bas-eke මේ බස්-එකෙ ‘this bus’.

[page 4]

3. THE PLURAL

The plural forms for bas-ekə බස්-එක and TikaT-eka ටිකට්-එක are bas බස් 'buses' and TikəT ටිකට් ‘tickets’. This “no ending” plural is the usual type for nouns with a definite form in -eke - එකෙ .

The plural of rupiyələ රුපියල is rupiyal රුපියල් ‘rupees’. Notice that there is again no ending. Also, we have al before endings, but -al -අල් when final. This is a regular change in Sinhalese; where a occurs before a consonant when something else follows, it becomes a if that consonant becomes final.

Place names like koləm̆bə කොලඹ and kalutərə කලුතර do not generally have plurals. The plurals of pronouns like meekə මේක will be taken up as they occur later.

4. CASES

Three of the four Sinhalese cases will be introduced here,

4.1 The Direct Case

The “basic” forms that have been given above and referred to simply as “singular definite,” “singular indefinite” or “plural” are in the DIRECT CASE. Thus, bas-ekə බස් - එක , bas-ekak බස්-එකක්, bas බස්; TikæT-ekə ටිකැට්- එක , TikæT-ekak ටිකැට්- එකක්, TikæT ටිකැට්; rupiyələ රුපියල, rupiyəlak රුපියලක්; kalutərə කලුතර, koləm̆bə කොලඹ and meekə මේක are all direct case forms, although some are singular, some plural, some definite and some indefinite.

When new nouns occur, and when they are listed in the glossary, they are always listed under the direct singular definite form.

The subject of most sentences is in the direct case:

bas-ekak tiyenəwa.
බස් - එකක් තියෙනවා .
There is a bus.

bas-ekə yanəwa.
බස් - එක යනවා .
The bus is going.

meekə yanəwa.
මේක යනවා .
This one is going.

5. NOUN CITATION

Since the definite—indefinite distinction operates only in the singular, we need specify only:

1. case

2. definite, indefinite or plural

in order to describe a noun form. That is, if we say that a noun form is either definite or indefinite it will automatically be singular as well. We will thus refer to nouns as being “dative definite” (automatically singular), “instrumental indefinite” (automatically singular), singular), “direct plural” (definite-indefinite does not apply), etc.

6.2 Uses of the Forms

6.21 The simple and emphatic present forms are both used in forming present tense sentences. The most common difference between statements formed with these two forms is in emphasis. Generally non-emphatic statement sentences are formed by using the simple present form.

[page 7]

mee bas-ekə kalutərəTə yanəwa.
මේ බස් - එක කලුතරට යනවා .
This bus is going to Kalutara.

meekə dæŋ yanǝwa.
මේක දැං යනවා .
This one is going now.

Where special emphasis is to be given like that which might be indicated by translation such as: “It is to Kalutara that this one is going” or “This one is going to Kalutara,” the emphatic form is commonly used. Frequently, though not invariably, the empha-sized part is placed after the verb:

mee bas-ekə yanne kalutərəTə
මේ බස් - එක යන්නේ කලුතරට
It is to Kalutara that bus is going.

koləm̆bəTə yanne meekə
කොලඹට යන්නේ මේක
This one is going to Colombo.

meekə yanne dæn də?
මේක යන්නේ දැන් ද ?
Is this one going now?

Constructions with the simple form of the verb will be referred to as SIMPLE STATEMENTS; those with the emphatic form of the verb as EMPHATIC STATEMENTS.

6.22 One of the most common uses of the INFINITIVE is as an imperative, in giving directions or orders. It usually occurs last in the sentence when so used:

meekeŋ kalutərəTə yannə.
මේකෙං කලුතරට යන්න .
Go to Kalutara by this one.

TikæT-ekak dennə.
ටිකැට් - එකක් දෙන්න .
Give (me) a ticket.

6.3 Agreement

Note that the verb forms above do not change to agree with the singular, plural, definite or indefinite status of nouns. For example:

mehee bas tiyenəwa.
මෙහේ බස් තියෙනවා .
There are buses here.

mehee bas-ekə tiyenəwa.
මෙහේ බස් - එක තියෙනවා .
Here is the bus -or- the bus is here.

mehee bas-ekak tiyenəwa.
මෙහේ බස් - එකක් තියෙනවා .
There is a bus here.

8. THE QUESTION MARKER də ද

də ද , THE QUESTION MARKER, may be added to any statement to change it to a question, but its placement varies with the kind of statement.

8.1 In a simple statement (i.e. one with the simple form of the verb), də ද always appears immediately after the verb:

koləm̆bəTə bas-ekak tiyenəwa.
කොලඹට බස් - එකක් තියෙනවා .
There is a bus to Colombo.

koləm̆bətə bas-ekak tiyenəwa də?
කොලඹත බස් - එකක් තියෙනවා ද ?
Is there a bus to Colombo?

8.2 In an emphatic statement (i.e. one with an emphatic form of the verb) də ද immediately follows the emphasized word, and cannot immediately follow the verb:

mee bas-ekə yanne kalutərəTə
මේ බස් - එක යන්නේ කලුතරට
This bus is going to Kalutara

mee bas-ekə yanne kalutərəTə də?
මේ බස් - එක යන්නේ කලුතරට ද ?
Is this bus going to Kalutara?

8.3 In all but a very few rare cases, then, following rule will hold for the position of də ද:

də ද always follows a simple verb; it never directly follows an emphatic verb.

9. INTERROGATIVE EXPRESSIONS

KohaaTə කොහාට ‘where to’ and kiiyə කීය ‘how much, how many’ are the first two of a set of interrogative words. In interrogative sentences, these appear with the question marker də ද. The də ද usually immediately follows the interrogative word, so that combinations such as kiiyə də කීය ද, kohaaTə də කොහාට ද are most easily learned as idiomatic idiomatic units. These interrogative expressions composed of interrogative plus an immediately following də ද never occur with a simple verb.

kohaaTə də කොහාට ද may be used without the emphatic form of the verb if the context is sufficiently clear without it.

meekə yanne kohaaTə də?
මේක යන්නෙ කොහාට ද ? or
meekə kohaaTə də?
මේක කොහාට ද ?
Where is this one going?

kiiyə də කීය ද is the usual expression for asking the price of something:

kalutərəTə kiiyə də?
කලුතරට කීය ද ?
How much to Kalutara?

TikæT-ekə kiiyə də?
ටිකැට් - එක කීය ද ?
How much is the ticket?
