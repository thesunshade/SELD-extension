3. PAST PARTICIPIAL CLAUSES

More than one past participial clause may occur with a single main verb. Usually a sequence of actions is involved, and commor English equivalents are such sentences as 'I go home, eat dinner and go to bed' or 'after going home and eating dinner, I'll go to bed'.

මදින මිනිහා ගහට නැගලා මල අග ටිකක් කපලා මුට්ටියක් එල්ලනවා.
The man who is tapping climbs the tree, cuts the flower, and hangs a pot.

[page 117]

මම ගෙදර ගිහිල්ලා කෑම කාලා ආපහු එන්නම්.
I'll come back after going home and eating dinner.

බංඩා වත්තට ගිහිල්ලා පොල් කඩලා වැටක් බැඳලා ගෙදර ගියා.
Banda went to the estate, picked coconuts, built a fence, and went home.

4. NUMERAL EXPRESSIONS

4.1 සැරේ and පාර are synonyms meaning 'time, occasion, instance'. They both occur in the construction "Numeral stem plus indefinite noun" (27, 6) to express 'two times', 'three times', etc.

දවසට දෙසැරයක් හැම ගහකටම නගින්න ඕන.
දවසට දෙපාරක් හැම ගහකටම නගින්න ඕන.
Twice a day you have to climb each tree.

මේ බේත දවසට තුන් සැරයක් කෑමට පස්සේ බොන්න.
මේ බේත දවසට තුන් පාරක් කෑමට පස්සේ බොන්න.
Take this medicine three times a day after meals.

4.2 Although only a limited number of nouns may occur in the construction "Numeral stem plus indefinite noun," as pointed out in Lesson 27, almost all nouns can occur in a similar construc-tion with the numeral 1, the indefinite noun 3 and an indefinite noun. Note that means 'a man' and when used with 'one man': that is එක මිනිහෙක් means

එක මිනිහෙක් one man

එක ගහක් one tree

එක පාරක් one time, one instance

In emphatic sentences, when the indefinite noun with or without is emphasized, the assertion marker replaces the indefinite ending and the meaning is 'only':

මට ඕන රුපියලයි.
I want (only) a rupee.

මට ඕන එක රුපියලයි.
I want only one rupee.

5. 'SIMILAR TO'

සමාන is used as a postposition with a dependent noun in the dative case meaning 'similar to, like'. The phrases formed in this way act as adjectives:

රා බරවලට සමානයි.
Toddy is similar to beer.

මේ ‍නෝනා අපේ අම්මාට සමානයි.
This woman is like my mother.

අරකට සමාන පොතක් ඕන.
I want a book similar to that one.

මට අපේ මාමාට සමාන මහත්තයෙක් හම්බ උනා.
I met a gentleman like my uncle.

එක The phrase

'alike'. like ໖໖ ໑໖ means the same as 'identical, Both phrases may act as adjectives:

මේ ලමයි දෙන්නා එක සමානයි.
මේ ලමයි දෙන්නා එක වගෙයි.
These two boys are alike.

ඇමෙරිකාවෙ මිනිස්සුයි ඉංගිරිසි මිනිස්සුයි එක සමානයි.
ඇමෙරිකාවෙ මිනිස්සුයි ඉංගිරිසි මිනිස්සුයි එක වගෙයි.
Americans and Englishmen are alike.

However, only a not ໖໖ ຜາ may be used with a verb:

මේ පොත් දෙක මට පේන්නෙ එක වගෙයි.
These two books look alike to me.

මේ ලමයි දෙන්නාට එක වගේ වැඩ කරන්න බෑ
These two boys can't work alike.

6. 8, 2

When 'this' and 'that' occur before the postposition ලඟ 'near', they take the forms and respectively. These phrases have the meaning 'next to this', 'next to that':

මී ලග සුමානෙ මට පවුල බලන්න යන්නම්.
Next week I'm going to see my family.

මී ලග හෝටලේට යන්න.
Go to the hotel next to this one.

[page 119]

වාමාගෙ කඩේට යන්න එපා.

ඊ ලඟ

කඩේට යන්න.

Don't go to (your) uncle's shop. Go to the one next to it.

In the dative case, these expressions are used as adverbs meaning 'next' (after this, after that).

ඊ ලඟට මම ගෙදර ගියා.
Next (after that) I went home.

ට ලඟට පස්වෙනි පාඬම කියවමු.
Next (after this) let's read the fifth lesson.

7. 'GET A CHANCE TO

හම්බ වෙනවා 'meet', with a dependent infinitive and a subject in the dative case, has the usual English equivalent 'get a chance to', 'have the opportunity to':

මට මේ සුමානෙ කොහේවත් යන්න හම්බ වෙන්නෙ නෑ.
This week I won't get a chance to go anywhere.

ඔහේට පැනි කන්න හම්බ උනා, නේ?
You have had a chance to eat treacle, haven't you?

8. 'CLIMB, DESCEND'

නගිනවා 'climb up', 'ascend', and ໑໙: 'climb down', 'descend' frequently imply a point to which or from which and hence usually occur with a noun or pronoun in either the dative or instrumental case:

ඔහේ ගහෙන් බහින්න.You come down from the tree!

ඔහේ මෙතෙන්ට බහින්න.Come down here!

ඔහේ බස් එකට නගින්න.You get into the bus.

මෙතනින් නගින්න පටන්ගන්න.Begin climbing from here.

9. බලනවා

බලනවා 'to see may have as object a clause with the verb in the simple form:

[page 120]

මම බංඩා වැටක් බඳිනවා බැලුවා.
I saw Banda build a fence.

එතකොට හකුරු හදනවාත් බලන්න පුලුවනි.
Then you can watch them make hakuru, too.

10. 'SEE'

The present tense simple and emphatic forms of the verb ເລິຫາ 'to see are used only in a few fixed idiomatic expressions. Instead, Sinhalese uses the verb as to express 'to see' in the present tense. In forms other than the present simple and present emphatic, either ເລິຫາ or පේනවා may be used with no difference in meaning. Note that ໑໙໖ always takes a dative case and ລິຫາ a direct case subject; but either verb may take a direct case noun or pronoun or a simple verb clause as object:

මට මිනිහෙක් එනවා පේනවා.
I see a man coming.

ඔහේට පේන්නෙ කොයි පන්සල ද?
Which temple do you see?

මම හකුරු හදනවා දැක්කා. මට හකුරු හදනවා පෙනුනා.
I saw hakuru being made.

11. 'FALSE'

The noun ລາ means 'the lie'. However, Sinhalese commonly uses the plural in situations where English would use the adjective 'false', as in the idiom 4, ເລ 4? 'true or false?'; literally 'the truth or lies?':

ඒ කතාව බොරු.
That story is false.

ඒක බොරු කතාවක්.
That is a false story.
