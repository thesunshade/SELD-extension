3. DATIVE CASE

3.1 අමාරු has the meaning 'difficult' when used with a subject in the direct case, but the meaning 'very 1ll' when used with a subject in the dative case:

මේ පොත අමාරුයි.
This book is difficult.

මහත්තයාට අමාරුයි.
The master is very ill.

තුවාල means that someone or something is injured when the subject is in the dative case. It means that a part of something

is injured with a subject in the direct case:

මහත්තයාට තුවාලයි.
He is injured.

කාර් එකට තුවාලයි.
The car is damaged.

අතක් තුවාලයි.
A hand (i.e., one of his hands) was injured.

දොරක් තුවාලයි.
A door was damaged.

[page 103]

3.2 In many sentences with adjectives like the following referr-ing to health or feelings, the subject is in the dative case:

මට මහන්සියි.
I'm tired.

මට සීතලයි.
I'm cold.

මට නිදිමතයි.
I'm sleepy.

මට බඩගිනියි.
I'm hungry.

මට වතුර තිබහයි.
I'm thirsty.

3.3 The same construction is used with nouns like the following referring to physical conditions:

මට හෙම්බිරිස්සාව.
I have a cold.

මට ඉසරදේ.
I have a headache.

මට මැලේරියාව.
I have malaria.

3.4 Getting an Illness

The names of illnesses (nouns) may occur with the Class 3 verb

: 'get (an illness)' and a subject in the dative case. When the verb is in the simple present, the sense is future; when it is in any past tense form, the sense is past:

දැන් ඇවිදින්න එපා, හෙම්බිරිස්සාව හැදෙනවා.
Don't go walking now, you'll get a cold.

මහත්තයාට හෙම්බිරිස්සාවක් හැදිලා.
The master has caught a cold.

මට ගිය සුමානෙ උන හැදුනා.
Last week I got a fever.

මහත්තයාට හැදුනෙ මැලේරියාව ලු.
It seems that it was malaria that the master got.

5. 'MUST HAVE'

'Must have' is expressed in Sinhalese by 48 plus a dependent infinitive and a subject in the direct case:

පීනසේන පනහට විතර යන්න ඇති.
Jinasena must have been going about 50.

ඊයෙ නම් යන්න ඇත්තේ ටිකක් කලබලෙන්.
Yesterday, of course, he must have been going a little fast.

6. COMPLETELY RUINED'

The expression ໑ ໑໙໖ ໑ means 'completely ruined', 'no good at all'. The item so described is usually in the genitive case, but may be in the instrumental:

[page 108]

කාර් එකේ නම් ගන්න දෙයක් නෑ.
The car, of course, was completely ruined.

මේ පොතේ ගන්න දෙයක් නෑ. මේ පොතෙන් ගන්න දෙයක් නෑ.
This book is no good at all.

7. හදනවා

The verb ໙໕໑໑ used with a dependent infinitive has the mean-ing 'get ready to', 'try to', 'pretend to'. The present form is likely to have the meaning 'get ready to', the past 'to try to', and the future 'to pretend to':

මාත් මේ යන්නයි හදන්නේ.
(Here) I am ready to go.

මහත්තයා වැඩ කරන්න හැදුවා.
He tried to work.

මහත්තයා යන්න හදාවී.
He might pretend to go.

8. ආරංචි

ආරංචි, which takes the assertion marker in simple sentences, means 'hear, get to know'. It is used with a subject in the dative case and with an object that is either a noun or pronoun in the direct case or a clause with (-3) ຜາ:

මට ආරංචියි වරද අනිත් මහත්තයාගෙයි කියලා.
I hear that the fault was the other man's.

ඒ කතාව මට ආරංචි උනා
I heard that story.

Note that, as in the last example, may occur with and past meaning.
