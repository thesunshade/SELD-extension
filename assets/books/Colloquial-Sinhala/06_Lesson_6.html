The noun wæDee වැඩේ ‘the work’ has the usual forms of a Class 4 noun, but it also has an irregular direct case indefinite form wæDak වැඩක්. eekə wæDak nææ.

That’s no use; There’s no use in that.’

2. CLASS 5 NOUNS

2.1 All nouns in Sinhala fall into two large divisions: ANIMATE and INANIMATE. Which division a given noun belongs to may be predicted almost without exception from its meaning.

1. INANIMATE: places, non-living things, plants

2. ANIMATE: persons, animals, living things (excluding plants)

There are a very few nouns, most of them referring to such things as groups or organizations of living things, which do not fit neatly into this classification.

The first four classes of Sinhala nouns (1-4) belong to the inanimate division whereas Class 5 belongs to the thematanimate division. The inflection of Class 5 nouns is illustrated by the following:

2.2 The Respectful Indefinite

Kinship terms specifying older relatives and certain nouns denoting superiors are respectful terms, and take a special indefinite. This is formed by adding kenek කෙනෙක්:

Definite

maama මාම

Indefinite

maama kenek මාම කෙනෙක්



In forming the remaining case forms, kenek කෙනෙක් is treated just like any Class 5 indefinite. Thus:

3. DIFFERENCES BETWEEN ANIMATE AND INANIMATE IN AGREEMENT

3.1 tiyenawə තියෙනවා - innəwa ඉන්නවා

tiyenəwa තියෙනවා and its various forms may be used only with inanimate objects. With animate subjects, the verb innəwa ඉන්නවා must be used in the same constructions:

[page 74]

bas tiyenawə.
බස් තියෙනවා .
There are buses.

lamay innəwa.
ලමය් ඉන්නවා .
There are children.

sigəræT tiyenne kaDee.
සිගරැට් තියෙන්නෙ කඩේ .
Cigarettes are in the shop.

lamea inne kaDee.
ලමයා ඉන්නෙ කඩේ .
The child is in the shop.

pawla පව්ල ‘the family’ is one of the few words that were mentioned in 3.1 as not falling neatly into the animate-inanimate classification. It is a regular Class 2 noun, and hence inanimate in inflection, but it takes innəwa ඉන්නවා:

magee pawlə inne gamee.
මගේ පව්ල ඉන්නෙ ගමේ .
My family is in the village.

When such nouns occur, the fact that they are inanimate but take innəwa ඉන්නවා will be stated.

5.2 ohee ඔහේ

The second person pronoun ohee ඔහේ which occurs in this lesson is used in some areas, areas, especially the South, to address equals or, as a polite form, to inferiors. However, the use of such pronouns may vary with the area of Sri Lanka. Thus ohee ඔහේ is not used in this way the up-country, and in some areas it may imply coldness, impersonality, or excessive distancing.

Thus, it is better to avoid ohee ඔහේ as a second person pronoun, unless one knows the usage in the particular place of speaking. In many areas, oyaa ඔයා, pl. oyaala ඔයාල, is coming into use as a kind of general, informal, second person pronoun for acquaintances, but again, it is best to be sure before using it. On the whole, the best strategy is to follow the common Sri Lankan usage, and use (1) nothing (a “zero” pronoun), (2) the person’s name, or (3) a title if appropriate. Thus, one could ask: banDaTə heTǝ ennə puluwaŋ də? බන්ඩට හෙට එන්න පුලුවන් ද?, mahatteaTə heTə ennə puluwaŋ də? මහත්තයට හෙට එන්න පුලුවන් ද? or, if the context will allow, simply: heTə ennə puluwaŋ də? හෙට එන්න පුලුවන් ද?

In any event, the use of pronouns is much less in Sinhala than in English, though doing without is sometimes a hard habit to acquire for English speakers.

The case forms of ohee ඔහේ and oyaa ඔයා are those of a Class 5 noun, The plurals are oheela ඔහේල and oyaala ඔයාල.

6. gamə ගම

The form game ගමෙ is used as an adverb of place equating with the English expression ‘at home’ when the latter means one’s native village or town, but not when it means ‘in one’s own house. It thus serves the same function as a genitive of place. Compare:

[page 77]

taatta inne gamə.
තාත්ත ඉන්නෙ ගම .
Father is at home (in his native village or town).

taatta inne gamee.
තාත්ත ඉන්නෙ ගමේ .
Father is in the village (not necessarily his own)

7. ASSERTION MARKER

When adjectives like poDi පොඩි, ending in -i -ඉ, take the assertion marker, -y -ය් , the pronunciation is -ii ඊ.

poDi wattak tiyenəwa.
පොඩි වත්තක් තියෙනවා .
There is a small estate.

mee wattə poDii.
මේ වත්ත පොඩී .
This estate is small.

8. ‘FROM...ON’

Note that in̆dələ ඉඳල may be used with time to mean ‘from … on’:

maTə heTə in̆dəla ennə puluwəni.
මට හෙට ඉඳල එන්න පුලුවනි .
I can come from tomorrow on.

udee in̆dələ mamə gamee innəwa.
උදේ ඉඳල මම ගමේ ඉන්නව .
From the morning on, I am (will be) in the village.

Note also that where a particular time is mentioned, or made clear from the context, the present tense may have a future meaning, so that innəwa ඉන්නවා in the sentence above can be translated as ‘will be’. Similarly:

heTə udee mamə koləm̆bəTə yanawa.
හෙට උදේ මම කොලඹට යනවා .
Tomorrow morning I am going (or ‘will go’) to Colombo

9. THE mee මේ SET AS ADVERBS OF PLACE

The forms mee මේ, oya ඔය, ara අර, ee ඒ can be used adverbially referring to place: ‘here, there, over there, there (in question)’. This is mostly restricted to emphatic sentences, especially those with innəwa ඉන්නවා or tiyenəwa තියෙනවා which single out or identify something or someone:

arə tiyenne wæDapələ.
අර තියෙන්නෙ වැඩපල .
There is the workshop (over there).

arə enne Tæksiyak.
අර එන්නෙ ටැක්සියක් .
There comes a taxi.

mee inne magee putaa.
මේ ඉන්නෙ මගේ පුතා .
Here is my son.

10. MORE NEGATIVE FORMATIONS

10.1 The form nææ නෑ serves as the negative of innəwa ඉන්නවා ‘there is, here are’ as well as the negative of tiyenəwa තියෙනවා ‘there is, there are’:

mehee eloolu tiyenəwa.
මෙහේ එලෝලු තියෙනවා .
There are vegetables here.

mehee eloolu nææ.
මෙහේ එලෝලු නෑ .
There are no (or 'aren't any) vegetables here.

[page 78]

mehee lamay innəwa.
මෙහේ ලමය් ඉන්නවා .
There are children here.

mehee lamay nææ.
මෙහේ ලමය් නෑ .
There are no (or 'aren't any') children here.

10.2 The negative of a simple sentence with a verb other than innəwa ඉන්නවා or tiyenəwa තියෙනවා is formed by changing the verb to the emphatic form and adding nææ නෑ:

puttu denna iskoole yanəwa.
පුත්තු දෙන්න ඉස්කෝලෙ යනවා .
The two boys are going to school.

puttu denna iskoole yanne nææ.
පුත්තු දෙන්න ඉස්කෝලෙ යන්නෙ නෑ .
The two boys don't go to school (or 'aren't going to school').

I'm taking a car from Anuradhapura on.

මම අනුරාදපුරේ ඉඳල කාර් - එකක් ගන්නෙ නෑ .

10.3 Equational sentences with adjectives as predicate attributes may be negated by dropping the assertion marker and adding nææ නෑ:

ee minihage wæDə hon̆day.
ඒ මිනිහගෙ වැඩ හොඳ්‍ය .

12.2 Removing the Inherent Vowel

The inherent vowel is removed by adding ් or {{ ්}} to a consonant symbol depending on the shape of that symbol:

{{ ්}} is added to ට , බ, ම, and other consonants yet to be learned of that general (”circular”) shape:

ම් m

බ් b

් is added to all other consonant symbols:

ද් d

ර් r

ල් l

ය් y

ප් p

න් n

හ් h



When the inherent vowel is removed, the consonant symbol represents the consonant alone. Thus if a word ends in a consonant, the symbol representing that consonant must have the inherent vowel removed:

බස් bas (compare රස rasə)

ගල් gal (compare ගල galə)

Double consonants are indicated by writing a consonant symbol twice and removing the inherent vowel from the first one. The second retains the inherent vowel:

ගන්න gannə

12.4 The Symbol ා

The symbol ා following a consonant symbol represents either a or aa instead of the inherent vowel as follows:

1. If the consonant symbol is at the beginning or in the middle of a word, the vowel represented by ා is aa ආ:

පාර paarə

පලාත palaatə

රස්සාව rassaawe

තානායම taanaayəmə

2. At the end of a word, ා will represent either a අ or aa ආ as follows:

If the word has two syllables, and the first syllable has a short vowel followed by a single consonant, ා will represent aa ආ:

[page 81]

බලා balaa

Otherwise, it will represent a අ, regardless of the number of syllables:

යනවා yanewa

මාමා maama

තාත්තා taatta

3. When case or plural endings are added to Class 5 nouns spelled with a final ා , that sound that ා will represent the same it did when final. Note that this leads to exceptions to rule #1:

තාත්තාට taattaTə

මාමාට maamaTə

මාමාලා maamalə



12.5 The verb kərənəwa is spelled කරනවා. This is an exception to the rule that a consonant symbol at the beginning of a word has the inherent vowel a අ, and is true for all forms of that verb:

කරන්න kərənnə
