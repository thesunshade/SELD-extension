Notes on New Nouns

1. The nouns koliijiyə කොලීජිය and widyaale විද්‍යාලෙ may be roughly translated as ‘high school’, in that they refer to the school immediately preceding the university. The word iskoole ඉස්කෝලෙ generally refers to the elementary school, attended for seven years, approximately from age five to age twelve, and the koliijiyə කොලීජිය or widyaale විද්‍යාලෙ is attended for seven years after the iskoole ඉස්කෝලෙ. koliijiyə කොලීජිය is the normal colloquial term and widyaale is more literary, although it may be used in a proper name like wisaaka widyaale විසාක විද්‍යාලෙ, ‘Visaka High School’.

2. daruwa දරුව generally means ‘one’s own child’, lamea ලමයා, in addition to being a term of address (see below), means ‘child’ in general.

3. Note that although waDee වඩේ ‘the job, work’ has the regular dative form wæDeeTe වැඩේටෙ, the form for to work as in come to work’ is wæDəTə වැඩට.

4. kenek කෙනෙක් ‘a person’ is the same item that forms the respectful indefinite, but as ‘a person, someone’, it has no special connotations of respect.

3. lamea ලමයා

The form lamea ලමයා may be used referring to a person of either sex who is a younger relative or an unrelated younger person of inferior status.

The term lamea ලමයා or oya lamea ඔය ලමයා may be used as an equivalent of English ‘you’ as follows:

oyə lameatə kaar eləwannə puluwaŋ, nee dǝ? OR
ඔය ලමයත කාර් එලවන්න පුලුවන් , නේ ද ?
lameaTə kaar eləwannə puluwaŋ, nee dǝ?
ලමයට කාර් එලවන්න පුලුවන් , නේ ද ?
‘You can drive a car, can’t you?’

Note that the use of lamea ලමයා in referring to a younger person is similar to the use of mahattea මහත්තයා in referring to an equal or superior, although oya ඔය is less frequently used with mahattea මහත්තයා.

As we have seen (lesson 3), lamea ලමයා may be used as a term. of direct address (calling someone), in which case oyǝ ඔය cannot be used with it. In this usage it very clearly implies that the speaker is is of superior status or that the one addressed very young, so that one should be cautious in using it.

lamea mehee paləturu tiyenəwa də?
ලමයා මෙහේ පලතුරු තියෙනව ද ?
Boy (or girl), is there fruit here?

6. THE CONJUNCTION y … y යි … යි

In the sentence

noonay daruwoy gamə nawətinəwa.
නෝනයි දරුවොයි ගම නවතිනව .
(My) wife and children are staying home.

the y’s යි’s are not assertion but together form a conjunction ‘and’ joining noona නෝන ‘wife’ and daruwo දරුවො ‘children’. When two or more items are joined thus or in a longer series, y යි is added to each of them. When this y යි is added to an item ending in a vowel final ə අ or aa ආ become a අ, ee ඒ becomes e එ, and other vowels remain unchanged (as with the assertion marker, -i -ඉplus y ය් is pronounced -ii -ඊ). The y’s යි’s are added after any other endings that may be present. markers,

keselgeDiy elooluy gannə onnə.
කෙසෙල්ගෙඩියි එලෝලුයි ගන්න ඔන්න .
I want to buy bananas and vegetables.

mamay, lameay, mahatteay koləm̆bəTə yanawa.
මමයි , ලමයයි , මහත්තයයි කොලඹට යනව .
I, the child, and the master are going to Colombo.

mamə sumaaneak kolam̆bay, siigiriyay, anuraadəpurey, yaapəney balannə yanəwa.
මම සුමානයක් කොලඹයි, සීගිරියයි, අනුරාදපුරෙයි , යාපනෙයි බලන්න යනව.
I am going for a week to see Colombo, Sigiriya, Anuradhapura, and Jaffna.

mehee in̆dala kalutərəTay, koləm̆bəTay bas tiyenəwa.
මෙහේ ඉඳල කලුතරටයි, කොලඹටයි බස් තියෙනව.
There are buses from here to Kalutara and Colombo.

mahatteagey, magey TikæT dekə maTə denna.
මහත්තයගෙයි , මගෙයි ටිකැට් දෙක මට දෙන්න.
Give me the two tickets belong-ing to the master and me. (Literally: the master's and my two tickets.)

7. SENTENCES WITH AN ADVERBIAL EXPRESSION

Sentences in English that are composed of subject plus verb ‘to be’ plus an adverbial expression may have an equivalent in Sinhalese either with or without the verb tiyenəwa තියෙනවා or innawa ඉන්නවා.

taatta gamə.
තාත්ත ගම .
Father is at home.

taatta gamə innəwa.
තාත්ත ගම ඉන්නවා .
Father is (stays, lives, remains) at home.

taatta inne gamə.
තාත්ත ඉන්නෙ ගම .
Father is at home.

The sentence with the verb innəwa ඉන්නවා or tiyenəwa තියෙනවා indicates a more permanent state of things, and the verb may equate with an English ‘stay, remain, live’, whereas the sentence without the verb indicates a temporary state of affairs:

[page 92]

paləturu tiyenne alləpu kaDee.
පලතුරු තියෙන්නෙ අල්ලපු කඩේ .
Fruit is in the next shop (where it generally is).

paləturu allapu kaDee.
පලතුරු අල්ලපු කඩේ .
Fruit is in the next shop (at the moment, but this is not the ordinary state of affairs).

8. nee නේ AND nee də නේ ද

The forms nee නේ and nee də නේ ද may be added to the end of a Sinhalese sentence to form a question where the expected answer is a confirmation of the original statement. It equates with the English ‘isn’t it, aren’t you, doesn’t he’, etc.

mahattea kalutərəTə yanawa, nee (də)?
මහත්තයා කලුතරට යනව , නේ ( ද )?
The gentleman is going to Kalutara, isn’t he? or
You are going to Kalutara, aren’t you?

mehee æpəl tiyenəwa, nee (də)?
මෙහේ ඇපල් තියෙනව , නේ ( ද )?
There are apples here, aren’t there?

mehee æpəl nææ, nee (də)?
මෙහේ ඇපල් නෑ , නේ ( ද )?
There are no apples here, are there?

mahattea eloolu ganne arə kaDee, nee (də)?
මහත්තයා එලෝලු ගන්නෙ අර කඩේ , නේ ( ද )?
You buy vegetables at that shop, don’t you? or
The gentleman buys vegetables at that shop, doesn't he?

9.2 CONSONANT COMBINATIONS

Consonant combinations may be written in the same manner as double consonants, i.e. with the inherent vowel removed from the first consonant symbol:

ක්ස ksə

න්ද ndə



Vowel symbols are added to the second consonant symbol:

ක්සි ks ඉ

න්දි nd ඉ


Combinations with w

Where the first consonant in a combination is w ව්, it will sometimes be treated like any other consonant and written ව් , but it will also be found written as wu වු, i.e. වු. For many words, these will be alternated. For example, pawlə පව්ල may be written පව්ල or පවුල In other cases, however, one of these spellings will be regularly used, so that spellings of words with these combinations should be learned as they occur.
