2. VERBS

2.1 NEW VERBS:

geenǝwa ගේනව ‘bring’

nætiwenǝwa නැතිවෙනව ‘get lost, not exist’

bedənəwa බෙදනව ‘distribute, deliver (mail)’

2.2 PASSIVE EQUIVALENT: ‘THEY DO’

bedənəwa බෙදනවා is a transitive verb, though bedanne nææ බෙදන්නෙ නෑ in the conversation is translated as the passive ‘aren’t delivered.’ Colloquial Sinhala has no real passive equivalent for the English passive (‘is done’, etc.). Instead, it commonly uses the ordinary verb with no subject expressed, as in this example. This is actually a kind of equivalent for English sentences with a non-specific ‘they’ as in ‘They (people in general) do that a lot now’. However, Sinhala never expresses the ‘they’ in this construction, but uses a “zero pronoun”. Thus:

mee wæDəpəlee huŋ̆gak wæDə kərənəwa, nee da?
මේ වැඩපලේ හුඟක් වැඩ කරනවා , නේ ද ?
‘They (people) do a lot of work in this workshop, don’t they.’ OR
‘A lot of work is done in this workshop, isn’t it?’

3. ‘HAVE, HAS’

3.1 The Sinhala construction for ‘have, has’ (possession) is formed with the dative case of the possessor, the direct case of the thing possessed, and the verb tiyenəwa තියෙනවා or innəwa ඉන්නවා. Thus,

maTə sigəræt tiyenəwa.
මට සිගරැත් තියෙනවා .
I have cigarettes.

Literally, this is To/for me there are cigarettes’.

mahatteaTə kaar-ekak tiyenəwa
මහත්තයට කාර් - එකක් තියෙනවා
The gentleman has a car.

arə noonaTə putek innəwa.
අර නෝනට පුතෙක් ඉන්නවා .
That lady has a son.

[page 103]

Notice that the choice of innəwa ඉන්නවා or tiyenəwa තියෙනවා depends upon whether the thing possessed is animate or inanimate, regardless of the possessor.

As with other constructions with these verbs, the negative is nææ නෑ substituted for the simple form:

maTə sigəræT nææ.
මට සිගරැට් නෑ .
I don't have cigarettes.

mahatteaTə kaar-ekak nææ.
මහත්තයට කාර් - එකක් නෑ .
The gentleman does not have a car.

arə noonaTə putek nææ.
අර නෝනට පුතෙක් නෑ .
That lady doesn't have a son.

This ‘have’ construction may have its literal meaning also, depending upon context, so that

maTə liyuŋ tiyenawa.
මට ලියුම් තියෙනව .

maTə liyuŋ nææ.
මට ලියුම් නෑ .

may mean either ‘I have letters’, ‘I don’t have letters’, or ‘There are letters for me’, ‘There are no letters for me.’

Hence,

maTə adə liyun nædda?
මට අද ලියුම් නැද්ද ?
Aren't there any letters for me today?

may also mean ‘Haven’t I any letters today?’

3.2 Instead of the construction with the dative case and tiyenəwa තියෙනවා or innawa ඉන්නවා, Sinhalese may use the construction with laŋ̆ge ලඟ that was met in Lesson 2. This is likely to equate with an English ‘with one’, ‘on one’, ‘at my place’, ‘here’:

mamə laŋ̆ga sigəræT tiyenəwa.
මම ලඟ සිගරැට් තියෙනවා .
I have cigarettes on me.

api laŋ̆gə hon̆də suruTTu tiyenəwa.
අපි ලඟ හොඳ සුරුට්ටු තියෙනවා .
We have good cigarettes here (i.e. with us, at our place, at our shop).

4. æti ඇති ‘MIGHT BE, MIGHT HAVE’

There are in Sinhalese constructions identical to those in Section 3, or to ‘there is, there are’, constructions, but with æti ඇති replacing innəwa ඉන්නවා, tiyenəwa තියෙනවා. These constructions imply ‘might be, might have, but with the implication that the situation discussed is fairly likely to be true or to come about.

[page 104]

saməharəwiTə heTə æti.
සමහරවිට හෙට ඇති .
Perhaps there might be some tomorrow.

dæŋ bas æti.
දැන් බස් ඇති .
There might be buses now.

maTə sigəræT æti.
මට සිගරැට් ඇති .
I might have cigarettes.

mahatteaTə kaar-ekak æti.
මහත්තයට කාර් - එකක් ඇති .
The gentleman might have a car.

arə noonaTə putek æti.
අර නෝනට පුතෙක් ඇති .
That lady might have a son.

api laŋ̆ge sigeræT æti.
අපි ලඟෙ සිගෙරැට් ඇති .
We might have cigarettes (on us).

5. THE VERBAL ADJECTIVE FORM OF THE VERB

A new verb form occurs in Conversation 8—the VERBAL ADJECTIVE. This is made by adding -nə -න to the stem of the verb. Stem-final -ə - අ is not changed before this ending, although it becomes -a- -අ- before the -nnə -න්න of the infinitive. This means that the verbal adjective will differ from the infinitive in these ways:

1. The verbal adjective adds -nə -න and the infinitive adds -nnə -න්න.

2. Stem-final -ə- -අ - is not changed before the -nə -න ending, but it becomes a අ before -nnə -න්න .

6. PRESENT TEMPORAL GERUND



Sinhalese forms ‘when’ clauses by using the verbal adjective with -koTə -කොට added to it: The verbal adjective plus koTə is referred to as the PRESENT TEMPORAL GERUND.

sanduda enəkoTə rupiyal pahee muddəreak geennə.
සන්දුද එනකොට රුපියල් පහේ මුද්දරයක් ගේන්න .
When you come on Monday, bring a five-rupee stamp.

[page 105]

siigiriyəTə yanəkoTə kaar-ekak gannə.
සීගිරියට යනකොට කාර් - එකක් ගන්න .
When you go to Sigiriya, take a car.

bas-ekee innəkoTə suruTTu bonnə epaa.
බස් - එකේ ඉන්නකොට සුරුට්ටු බොන්න එපා .
Don't smoke cigars when (you are) on the bus.

mahattea siigiriyəTə yanəkoTə mamə kaar-ekə eləwənəwa.

මහත්තයා සීගිරියට යනකොට මම කාර් - එක එලවනවා .
When the master goes to Sigiriya. I drive the car.

7. THE CONSTRUCTION -y … -у -යි … -යි

When the conjunction -y … -у -යි … -යි is added to a word that ends in a consonant, -u- -උ- is added before the -y -යි.

putekuy duwekuy
පුතෙකුයි දුවෙකුයි
a son and a daughter

bas-ekǝkuy Tæksiyəkuy
බස් - එකකුයි ටැක්සියකුයි
a bus and a taxi

8. witərə ‘ONLY’ AND witəray ‘ABOUT’

witərə 'only' contrasts with witərə 'about' in that witərə 'only' will appear in the indefinite form when non-final in a sentence, and will take the assertion marker -y when final in an emphatic sentence, whereas witərə 'about almost always appears without any ending in both situations. Both forms commonly occur with quantity phrases, and then follow the entire phrase. The noun or numeral preceding either witərə 'only or witərə'about may be definite, indefinite or plural and in any case that the construction requires:

mamə sumaaneak witərə wæDə kərənəwa.

I am working for about a week.

mamə sumaaneak witərak wæDə kərənəwa.

I am working for only a week.

mamə koləm̆bə yanne heTǝ witerǝ.

It's about tomorrow that I'm going to Colombo (i.e. tomorrow or so').

[page 106]

mamə koləm̆bə yanne heTə witəray.

It's only tomorrow that I'm going to Colombo.

keselge Di pahak witərə geenne.

Bring about five bananas.

keselge Di pahak witərak geenna.

Bring only five bananas.

mahattea koləm̆bəTə witere bas-eken yanawa.

The master is going about (as far as) Colombo by bus.

mahattea koləm̆bəTə witərak bas-eken yanawa.

The master is going only to Colombo by bus.
