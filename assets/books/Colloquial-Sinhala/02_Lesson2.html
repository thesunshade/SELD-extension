    1. Grammar

1. ADDRESS

mahattea මහත්තයා is used to address or refer to either (male) strangers 1 or superiors, excluding relations and clergy. Servants use mahattea මහත්තයා to refer to or address their employers, and thus it is generally translated (the) master'. Elsewhere, as when used for strangers, it means something like 'the gentleman' or ‘sir’, although there is no real English equivalent. It is the most commonly used polite form of address, and such terms are mandatory in Sinhalese in many situations in which a term of deference like “sir” would be optional or not used at all in English. Notice that in many situations where English would have the pro-noun "you" Sinhalese uses a form like mahattea මහත්තයා: e.g. mahattea suruTTu bonewa də? මහත්තයා සුරුට්ටු බොනෙවා ද? 'Do you smoke cigars? (Literally: Does the gentleman smoke cigars?).

2. NOUN CLASSES

On the basis of the inflectional form of nouns, they may be grouped into four classes:

1. Those with a direct definite form in -ekə -එක

2. Those with a direct definite form in a and an instrumental definite in -en -එන්

3. Those with a direct definite in a and an instrumental in -iŋ -ඉන්

4. Those with a direct definite form in -ee -ඒ or -e -එ

These classes will be referred to as classes 1, 2, 3 and 4 respectively.

3.5 Use of the Genitive

1. A noun or pronoun dependent on the postposition in̆dəla ඉඳල will always precede in̆dəla ඉඳල and will be in the genitive case:

kalutərə in̆dəla කලුතර ඉඳල

from Kalutara

laŋkaawe in̆dəla ලංකාවෙ ඉඳල

from Ceylon

meeke in̆dəla මේකෙ ඉඳල

from this one



2. The genitive case may be an equivalent of the English possessive -’s construction or “of” construction:

mee suruTTuwe suwən̆də
මේ සුරුට්ටුවෙ සුවඳ
this cigar's aroma, or
the aroma of this cigar

With place names, it may equate with the English adjectival form of the place name:

maatəlee suruTTu
මාතලේ සුරුට්ටු
Matale cigars

laŋkaawe sigəræT
ලංකාවෙ සිගරැට්
Ceylonese cigarettes

6. NEAR

laŋ̆gə ලඟ ‘near, with by (location)’ is a postposition taking the noun or pronoun dependent on it in the direct case:

api laŋ̆gə
අපි ලඟ
with us

arə bas-ekə laŋ̆gə
අර බස් - එක ලඟ
by (near) that bus

Note that a phrase with laŋ̆gə ලඟ together with the verb tiyenəwa තියෙනවා may have the English equivalent ‘have’. Thus:

api laŋ̆gə hon̆də suruTTu tiyenəwa.
අපි ලඟ හොඳෙ සුරුට්ටු තියෙනවා .
We have good cigars (literally ‘By us there are good cigars’).

7. EQUATIONAL SENTENCES

In Equational sentences are those in which one thing is equated with another, e.g. English “x is y.” In such a sentence the x may be referred to as subject and y as predicate attribute. In Sinhalese, there is no equivalent of the English “is” and the structure of the sentence depends on whether the predicate attribute is an adjective or noun.

7.1 With an Adjective as Predicate Attribute

Adjectives such as rasə රස ‘tasty’, særə සැර ‘strong (as of tobacco)’ and hon̆də හොඳ ‘good’, which end in a vowel, add -y - යි when they appear as predicate attributes (note that ə අ becomes a අ before final y යි):

mee sigəræT hon̆day.
මේ සිගරැට් හොඳයි
These cigarettes are good.

maatəlee suruTTu bohomə særay.

7.2 With a Noun as Predicate Attribute

When the predicate attribute is a noun, there is no y යි added. Both subject and predicate attribute are in the direct case:

mee triiroos.
මේ ත්‍රීරෝස් .
These are Three Roses.

mee bas-ekə koləm̆bə bas-ekak.
මේ බස් - එක කොලඹ බස් - එකක් .
This bus is a Colombo bus.

8. THE LOCATION SETS

mee මේ ‘this’, arə අර ‘that’ and meekə මේක ‘this one’ are representatives of two sets of items that are part of a large and important group of similar sets involving differences in location. Only the first two sets will be given here.

8.1 The ‘mee’ ‘මේ’ set

The forms in this set are mee මේ, oyə ඔය, arə අර and ee ඒ. There are four distinctions in location where English has two, and these remain the same throughout the rest of the sets, so that this set should be learned thoroughly first. The first three forms in this set involve distinction in location with respect to speaker and hearer; the fourth refers to the topic being discussed.

[page 21]

1. mee මේ ‘this, these’; near the speaker

2. oyə ඔය ‘that, those’; near the hearer

3. arə අර ‘that, those’; away from both speaker and hearer, but in sight. This implies “pointing at” roughly ‘that (those) over there’

4. ee ඒ ‘that, those’; away from both speaker and hearer, out of sight or with no “pointing at” implied; roughly, ‘that (those) we are talking about’ or ‘that (those) in question’

The The form mee මේ will be translated as “this” or “these”. Other forms will normally all equate with English “that” or those” ‘, but where it is important to indicate which form is meant, e.g. in exercises, oya ඔය will be translated as “that (those) near you”; arə අර as “that (those) over there’; and ee ඒ as “that (those) in question”. It is important to remember that this distinction is a necessary one in Sinhalese even though the last three forms will all equate with English “that, those”.

The members of the mee මේ set never change form, regardless of the case or number of the nouns they precede. They may thus be “this (that)” or “these (those)” depending upon context:

mee bas-ekə
මේ බස් - එක
this bus

arə bas-ekəTə
අර බස් - එකට
(to) that bus

ee bas
ඒ බස්
those buses

8.3 The Uses of the Sets

8.31 The characteristic use of the mee මේ set is to qualify following nouns:

[page 22]

mee bas-ekə
මේ බස් - එක
this bus

arə TikæT-ekə
අර ටිකැට් - එක
that ticket

ee pækæT-ekə
ඒ පැකැට් - එක
that pack

Notice that when the members of this set are used in this way, the noun qualified is always in the definite or the plural, never the indefinite.

8.32 The members of the meekə මේක set are pronouns that substitute for one of the mee මේ set plus definite noun:

mee TikæT-ekə rupiyal dekay.
මේ ටිකැට් - එක රුපියල් දෙකයි .
This ticket is two rupees.

meekə rupiyal dekay
මේක රුපියල් දෙකයි
This one is two rupees.

ee bas-ekə maatəleeTə yanəwa.
ඒ බස් - එක මාතලේට යනවා .
That bus is going to Matale.

eekə maatəleeTə yanəwa.
ඒක මාතලේට යනවා .
That one is going to Matale.
