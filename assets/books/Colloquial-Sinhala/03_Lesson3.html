3. ‘MADAM’

noona නෝන and noona mahattea නෝන මහත්තයා, like mahattea ,මහත්තයා are used either as a term of address or as an equivalent of English ‘you’, but for women rather than men. Both noona නෝනand noona mahattea නෝන මහත්තයා are respectful terms, and one of them would almost always be used by a servant, shopkeeper, a bus conductor in addressing a lady. However, noona mahattea නෝන මහත්තයා implies greater respect than just noona නෝන , and so would scarcely ever be used where the person addressed was considered to be of equal or inferior status. Simple noona නෝන, however, like mahattea මහත්තයා, may be used for equals and, if desired, inferiors when they are strangers. In circumstances where greater familiarity exists, equals and inferiors are generally addressed by their proper names.

noona mahatteaTə paləturu oonə də?
නෝන මහත්තයට පලතුරු ඕන ද ?
Do you want fruit, madam?

noona mee bas-ekeŋ yanəwa də?
නෝන මේ බස් - එකෙන් යනවා ද ?
Are you going by this bus?

4. THE GENITIVE OF PLACE

One of the characteristic uses of nouns in the genitive case in Sinhalese is adverbial, to indicate location. The sense is generally ‘in’ or ‘at’ whatever is referred to by the noun (or pronoun) in the genitive. Thus, in the sentence

paləturu tiyenne allǝpu kaDee.
පලතුරු තියෙන්නෙ අල්ලපු කඩේ .
Fruit is in the adjoining shop

kaDee කඩේ is in the genitive case, although with this noun (as with maatəlee මාතලේ) the genitive and direct are the same in form. Compare the following:

sigəræT tiyenne pækæT-ekee.
සිගරැට් තියෙන්නෙ පැකැට් - එකේ .
The cigarettes are in the packet.

bas-ekə arəke tiyenəwa.
බස් - එක අරකෙ තියෙනවා .
The bus is in that one (such as a building).

ee kaDee tiyenne kalutərə.
ඒ කඩේ තියෙන්නෙ කලුතර .
That shop is in Kalutara (genitive).

mahattea paləturu ganne ee kaDee.
මහත්තයා පලතුරු ගන්නෙ ඒ කඩේ .
The master buys fruit at that shop (genitive).

They that appears on adjectives used as predicate attributes is the assertion marker. It has a number of important functions; those that have appeared so far are listed here.

1. It appears (as pointed out in Lesson 2) on adjectives used as predicate attributes in equational sentences, provided the adjective ends, as all have so far, in a vowel:

This one is very good.

mee keselgeDi rasay.
මේ කෙසෙල්ගෙඩි රසයි .

7. ekak එකක් ‘each’ (literally ‘a one’)

The numeral ‘one’ is eka එක. The indefinite form of eka එක ‘one’ is ekak එකක්, literally ‘a one’. It is used to mean ‘each’, as in:

meekə ekak sata tunay.
මේක එකක් සත තුනයි .
This one (i.e. ‘This bunch’) is three cents each.

The indefinite forms of the other numerals may be used in the same way, with the meaning ‘for two’, ‘for three’, etc. Thus:

meekə dekak satə tunay.
මේක දෙකක් සත තුනයි .
This one (i.e. This bunch’) is three cents for two.

meekə tunak satə tunay.
මේක තුනක් සත තුනයි .
This one is three cents for three.

meekə hatərak satə tunay.
මේක හතරක් සත තුනයි .
This one is three cents for four.

8. THE ‘WANT’ CONSTRUCTION

The Sinhalese word onnə ඔන්න is an equivalent of English ‘want’. The person who wants something is in the dative case:

maTə … onnə
මට … ඔන්න
I want …

mahatteaTə … onnə
මහත්තයට … ඔන්න
The gentleman wants …

Like English ‘want’, onnə ඔන්න may occur with a dependent noun or verb.

1. A dependent noun is in the direct case:

mahatteaTə keselgeDi oona.
මහත්තයට කෙසෙල්ගෙඩි ඕන .
The gentleman wants bananas.

[page 34]

maTə paləturu onnə.
මට පලතුරු ඔන්න .
I want fruit.

2. A dependent verb is always in the infinitive form:

mahatteaTə yannə onnə.
මහත්තයට යන්න ඔන්න .
The gentleman wants to go.

The infinitive may in turn have its own object or modifiers (as may the equivalent English infinitive). Note the order:

maTə paləturu gannə onnə.
මට පලතුරු ගන්න ඔන්න .
I want to buy fruit.

mahatteaTə kalutərəTə yannə onnə.
මහත්තයට කලුතරට යන්න ඔන්න .
The gentleman wants to go to Kalutara.

9. SOME NEGATIVE FORMATIONS: nææ නෑ and epaa එපා

9.1 The negative of tiyenəwa තියෙනවා ‘there is’ is nææ නෑ, substituted for tiyenəwa තියෙනවා.

mehee æpel tiyenəwa.
මෙහේ ඇපෙල් තියෙනව .
There are apples here.

mehee æpəl nææ.
මෙහේ ඇපල් නෑ .
There aren't any (or 'are not') apples here.

9.2 -Tə … onnə ට … ඔන්න sentences, with either a noun or a verb, may be negated by adding nææ නෑ.

maTə keselgeDi onnə.
මට කෙසෙල්ගෙඩි ඔන්න .
I want bananas.

maTə keselgeDi onnə nææ.
මට කෙසෙල්ගෙඩි ඔන්න නෑ .
I don't want bananas.

maTə paləturu gannə onnə.
මට පලතුරු ගන්න ඔන්න .
I want to buy fruit.

maTə paləturu gannə onnə nææ.
මට පලතුරු ගන්න ඔන්න නෑ .
I don't want to buy fruit.

9.3 If the Tə … onnə ට … ඔන්න sentence has a dependent noun, it may also be negated in an alternate way: epaa එපා is substituted for onnə ඔන්න:

maTə keselgeDi oonə.
මට කෙසෙල්ගෙඩි ඕන .
I want bananas.

maTə keselgeDi oonə nææ. or
මට කෙසෙල්ගෙඩි ඕන නෑ .
maTə keselgeDi epaa.
මට කෙසෙල්ගෙඩි එපා .
I don't want bananas.

However, Tə … onnə ට … ඔන්න sentences with a dependent infinitive cannot be negated with epaa එපා, but only by adding nææ නෑ.
